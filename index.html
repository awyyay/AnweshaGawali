My three laws

I’m into physics and maths, and it turns out my life follows the same rules.

1

Inertia: I stay where I am until I apply a force

I’ve been lazy and stuck. I over-planned instead of doing, polished videos instead of posting, and once closed a door myself, right before walking through it. Staying put never fixed it. A new force in a new direction did.

2

Force: challenge myself, daily

It started in Grade 8 with a month without my phone. My dad left it in my room in front of me and I never lifted it. It was the first time I controlled a craving, and I’ve challenged myself every day since. Taking it up is the hard part.

3

Reaction: every no pushes back, and teaches

Rejections, a stammer on stage, three programs that said no. Each one pushed back and sent me off in a better direction. Starting new motion while keeping the old motion going is how my life connects.

Now · Founder

Precise

What I’m doing

Building a single-dose skincare brand with my cofounder Daanish, whom I met at a founder program: sealed capsules, no water. I handle sales and growth strategy.

What’s hard

Getting sales as a young person who isn’t pursuing medical studies. It’s hard for people to believe in you.

What I’ve learned

To keep going even when others doubt me. Know your product and trust it, and others will trust it too. If I’m not confident yet, I fake it till I make it. I was sceptical about this at first, because people can have allergies and skin reactions, but you have to have faith in your product.

May 2026 · Grade 12 · Mesa FFP

Dusk, a perfume brand

What I did

I flew to Bangalore alone, knew no one, and two weeks later was selling perfume in a panda costume. With three teammates I built Dusk from scratch and owned decisions on pricing, marketing and shipping. We started as Mesa’s first batch, so honestly as guinea pigs, then found our idea: premium-inspired perfume at a lower cost, with our own twist on the scents, so an average man can try before buying an expensive original.

  • Built the website on Lovable; designed the logo, colours and brand in Canva
  • Made AI-generated ads and product images
  • B2B calls, the catalogue and offline sales
  • Filmed the whole journey, even when I was the only one recording
What went wrong

On Mega Flea Day our products and stickers hadn’t arrived, and we faced 29 other stalls, some also selling perfume. A mentor suggested opening our website on laptops so people could try samples and order directly. I borrowed a panda suit from a neighbouring stall to pull customers in, and my dry ice idea worked too.

What I learned

People buy proof, not promises. Plans collapse, so adapt. Fall in love with the problem, not the idea. Rejections are the first step to yes.

Grades 9–10 · RISE

The Silent Gamble

What I did

Applied to RISE and changed my project into a community about how small decisions, like how early you wake up or whether you procrastinate, shape a whole life. I wrote, filmed and edited everything, researched and set up interviews with handpicked peers, and got help from a TEDx speaker, past finalists and global winners. When I met RISE selection director Muloongo, she noticed my curiosity and let me host her AMA with two teammates, on Zoom, with 15 people attending live and a Discord community of 60.

What went wrong

Before this, I’d scripted a speech tour, got three top Pune schools to agree, then backed out of the first out of fear. How do I teach students confidence if I’m scared myself? The biggest mistake of my life. I wasn’t selected, and I know I hadn’t given 100%. Later, interviews were hard to arrange and people bailed last minute.

What I learned

Things happen: you can get embarrassed, stumble or be roasted, and you only get past it by standing in it. I learned to love experimenting and failing, plus patience and empathy when I couldn’t follow someone’s English. I now go on treks to talk with younger kids, and I’m learning Marathi. I’m redesigning the channel around what’s sustainable.

Grades 9–10 · Leadership

Student Leadership Conference

What I did

In Grade 9 only student council members were meant to go. When teachers couldn’t help and the deadline neared, I went straight to the principal, who called the host in front of me and made a seat. I created that opportunity myself. In Grade 10 I was invited back to speak in front of around 300 people.

What went wrong

I had a skit script and a song made for my channel. Then I met the other speaker, who wasn’t using a skit, and went spontaneous. I stammered, missed my cue for the song, and everything I’d prepared fell apart. I stayed on stage anyway.

What I learned

Do what you do, and don’t copy others. Take the risk: you create your own luck.

Since I was small · Business

Selling things

What I did

Sold things I made at home to friends, who paid with pocket money. Then I pitched strangers in other apartment complexes. During the pandemic I opened a printing press in my complex, with my dad’s guidance and safety taken care of, because nothing was nearby. Later I painted and sold my own art, asking interior designers and artists how to sell.

What went wrong

I was nervous and awkward, and I got a lot of no’s. At first a no felt terrible.

What I learned

I’m not afraid of rejection any more. A no lets me move on and a yes pays, but a “maybe” eats my time. So I learned to get to a clear yes, no or not now.

Grades 8–12 · Personal challenge

Phone-free, then a year without social media

What I did

After the one-month phone challenge, I took a year without social media. Once I got my phone back I switched my icons to black and white, set a time at night to post and talk to friends, and added app blockers.

What went wrong

I was anxious, angry and sad, compared myself to peers on LinkedIn, and had FOMO. Then I started escaping life: swimming for hours, reading, sleeping, isolating myself.

What I learned

Waking early, showing up for friends and starting imperfectly helped. Doing was the only thing that ever got anything done. I’m writing a book about it.

Lab notes

Rules I learned by doing, some from watching others get burned.

MoneyRevenue is not profit. Get paid before you ship. I learned that last one watching a friend get scammed.
SalesListen first, then solve the other person’s problem. Be clear what you’re building, because if you’re confused, so is your customer.
LeadershipIt’s listening and clarity, not control. Teams need shared goals. Strategy is deciding what not to do.
PerfectionIt’s a myth. I used to over-edit videos and lost consistency. Now I start simple and upgrade as I go.

I also love photography. On a solo trip I wanted to ride and play in the snow, with no one to help me film on my camera, and I couldn’t decide whether to capture the moment or just feel it. Since then I document the places, the lessons and the people.
